=== METHODOLOGY ===

1. Information

The executor receives only the comment-free RTL of one hardware IP module. The RTL may declare more than one entity. The RTL is the sole authoritative source. Comments are not a source of evidence.

The executor distinguishes Explicit information, RTL-derived information, and External/contextual information, as defined in Section 5. Only explicit and RTL-derived information may be used to classify an asset. External/contextual information is never silently converted into an RTL fact. If security relevance depends on unavailable external information, that dependency is treated as Context-dependent, not assumed.

The analysis answers, in order:
- What is explicitly present in the RTL?
- What can be logically established from it?
- What remains unknown because it needs external context?

The executor maintains working evidence records but does not expose private chain-of-thought. Output reasoning consists of concise, auditable evidence summaries.

Asset identification remains separate from vulnerability discovery, exploitability analysis, and threat assessment. An attacker model or attack path is not required to recognize an RTL-established asset property.

2. Observation

First construct the closed set. Record each candidate’s exact declared name, declaring entity, declaration kind, direction if applicable, type, width, and identifiable internal record fields.

Apply the closed set literally:
- Include the declared ports of every entity, declared internal signals, and individually identifiable fields of internal record signals.
- Interpret a signal according to its RTL role, not solely its language declaration keyword.
- Do not create candidates for expressions, assignments, operators, processes, functions, procedures, conditions, state transitions, constants, type declarations, enumeration literals, instance names, arbitrary slices, or undeclared array entries. These constructs may provide evidence.
- Write internal record fields using the declared signal and field identifiers joined by their dots. Do not invent aliases, hierarchical instance names, or bit-field assets.
- Preserve declaring-entity identity even when an element connects to another entity.

Before classification, derive relationships using applicable RTL semantics:
- Driver and assignment relationships, including guarded assignments.
- Combinational data dependencies and transformations.
- Storage, update, hold, reset, and initialization behavior.
- State transitions and the conditions enabling them.
- Control dependencies, including selection, enablement, suppression, and completion conditions.
- Comparisons, checks, decisions, and events, together with their operands and consequences.
- Connections between supplied entities and propagation to their output-capable ports.
- Conditional visibility, reachability, and elaboration relationships where the RTL establishes them.

Respect assignment scheduling, clocking, widths, signedness, and other relevant language semantics. Use function or procedure bodies as evidence when present, without treating those constructs as assets.

Separate a value’s semantic origin or realization from intermediate transport. Record whether an element retains the value, establishes it, computes it, exports it, merely copies it, or gates its propagation. Assignment syntax alone does not establish a realization role.

Honor determinable elaboration conditions. Otherwise retain explicit conditions rather than selecting an unstated configuration. Do not invent behavior for unavailable entity implementations.

The stage output is a working relationship model connecting declared elements to identifiable values, decisions, states, computations, and outputs, with supporting RTL locations or constructs and any scope conditions.

3. Use-case flows

Derive use-case flows before identifying conceptual assets.

Recognize flows from connected RTL behavior: accepted inputs or requests, configuration updates, state progression, computations, checks, data retention, output production, status reporting, and reset or recovery operations. These are recognition patterns, not presumed operations.

A flow need not use a state machine or handshake. Combinational behavior can implement a flow. Examine local operations of every supplied entity as well as flows spanning their connections.

For each flow, record:
- Its operation, described using RTL-supported semantics.
- Its triggering conditions or, for continuously evaluated logic, its evaluation conditions.
- The meaningful values and state it consumes, produces, retains, or changes.
- The relevant computations, decisions, and state progression.
- Its observable result, completion condition, or continuing behavior.
- Explicit guards, exceptional paths, and conditions limiting the claim.
- The relationship evidence supporting the description.

Do not assign an application purpose or software interpretation merely because an operation resembles a familiar design pattern. Keep flows separate when they implement distinct operations; connect them where the RTL establishes a shared value or state.

4. External information

External conclusions include intended deployment, system-level privilege, data ownership, secrecy requirements, software usage, trust boundaries not represented in the RTL, attacker capabilities, external timing guarantees, and the behavior of missing implementations.

Identify the specific missing premise rather than filling it with convention.

Use Context-dependent when the RTL establishes a meaningful value, state, or decision but its security significance requires such a premise. Use Insufficient evidence when the conceptual meaning or relevant property itself cannot be established.

Do not assume:
- A name denotes a secret, permission, trusted domain, or security policy.
- Internal placement implies confidentiality.
- Configuration or control automatically implies security significance.
- An external participant behaves fairly or eventually responds.
- A familiar interface or computation has an unstated application purpose.
- An unavailable component implements a function suggested by its name.

Unknown behavior limits only the conclusions that depend on it. It does not erase independently established facts elsewhere in the RTL.

5. Asset definition

Apply these definitions:

- Two axes. Assets are classified along two independent axes: abstraction (conceptual vs structural, from IEEE P3164) and dependency (primary vs secondary, from SAIF).
- Conceptual asset: a piece of data, configuration, or system state (including a decision or event the module computes) that a use-case flow of the IP depends on, and whose confidentiality, integrity, or availability must hold for that flow to be secure. It is defined by what it means, not where it is. It must be identifiable from the RTL as a value the module produces, stores, or uses.
- Structural asset (structural reference): a declared RTL element (a port, a signal, or a field of an internal record signal) that realizes a conceptual asset: where its value is stored, set, or computed, or the port through which it leaves its entity. An element that only passes the value along, or gates it on the way, carries the asset but is not its structural reference. One conceptual asset usually has several structural references along its path, and one element can realize several conceptual assets.
- Primary asset: a primary asset is an element that stores, sets, or computes a security-relevant value or decision, or a port through which such a value leaves its entity. Elements that only pass the value along or gate it between those points are not primary, but secondary assets. At module level, the primary assets are the structural references of the conceptual assets. These are what the executor reports.
- Secondary asset: an element that only passes a primary asset's value along or gates it between those points, or an internal signal or register that influences a primary asset or could violate its security objective. The executor never reports secondary assets; a later stage finds them from connectivity.
- Use-case flow: an operation the RTL implements, such as configuring the module, starting an operation, computing a result, reporting status, or reading out data.
- Closed set (candidate universe): the declared ports of every entity in the RTL, the declared internal signals, and the individually identifiable fields of internal record signals. Nothing else can be a structural asset.
- Evidence levels:
  Established: the RTL explicitly or logically establishes that the value, state, or decision has a confidentiality, integrity, or availability property that matters to a module operation or flow.
  Context-dependent: the RTL identifies a meaningful value, state, or decision, but whether its security matters depends on information outside the RTL.
  Insufficient evidence: the RTL does not provide enough information to establish the conceptual meaning or the security property.
- Information categories:
  Explicit information: directly represented in the RTL, including declared ports, signals, fields, types, widths, element names, assignments, state elements, state transitions, and explicit operations or comparisons.
  RTL-derived information: not stated directly but logically established from the RTL, such as a value being stored in a register, computed from particular inputs, controlling a state transition, propagating to a particular output, or determining another state or value.
  External/contextual information: requires knowledge beyond the RTL, such as system architecture, application purpose, designer intent, software or firmware behavior, deployment environment, threat model, unstated security requirements, or how a signal is used outside the module.

Conceptual assets are identified first, from the use-case flows; structural references are identified second. Never start by labeling individual signals as assets. Report a conceptual asset only when its evidence level is Established.

Define each concept by its semantic value, state, decision, or event and its role in a flow. Do not create a separate concept merely because the same meaning appears at another location. Conversely, connectivity alone does not make different meanings the same concept.

6. Security relevance

For each proposed concept, identify a concrete property established by the RTL and explain why the flow depends on that property. Evaluate objectives separately:

- Confidentiality: unauthorized disclosure of the value would violate a property established from the RTL.
- Integrity: unauthorized modification, corruption, or incorrect setting of the value or decision would violate a property established from the RTL.
- Availability: preventing the value, state, decision, or event from being produced, maintained, or delivered would violate a property established from the RTL.

Use an evidence-to-property test:
- Identify the meaningful value, state, decision, or event in a derived flow.
- Identify the actual RTL-defined restriction, invariant, computation contract, state-validity condition, or production/delivery obligation.
- Explain how the corresponding disclosure, incorrect value, or suppression would violate that specific property.
- Confirm that this explanation does not require an unstated external premise.

An explicit security label or dedicated security mechanism is not required. A concrete RTL-defined computation or operation contract can establish integrity or availability. However, merely observing that a signal affects an output, changes a transition, has storage, or participates in control is not a complete security argument. The semantic value and violated flow property must be identified.

For confidentiality, establish a relevant non-disclosure or restricted-observation property, not merely internal placement or selective routing. Ordinary gating does not by itself establish secrecy.

For integrity, identify what must remain correct and the consequence for the implemented flow. Distinguish a meaningful decision or result from its incidental dependencies.

For availability, identify the production, maintenance, progression, or delivery obligation and the RTL conditions under which it holds. Do not invent deadlines, unconditional progress, environmental fairness, or external response guarantees.

Treat relevance as:
- Explicit when the relevant constraint or property is directly represented by RTL behavior.
- RTL-derived when relationships logically establish the property and its flow consequence.
- Context-dependent when establishing the security significance needs information outside the RTL.

Avoid generic assumptions such as “all configuration is sensitive” or “all control signals are security-critical.” Do not infer confidentiality from integrity or availability, or vice versa.

7. Evidence sufficiency

Every classification must be supported by RTL evidence.

Evidence is an explicit RTL fact or a conclusion that follows logically from identified RTL relationships. Inference is an interpretation the RTL does not establish.

For an Established conceptual asset, retain a sufficient chain:
RTL evidence → identifiable value, state, or decision → security significance for a derived flow → confidentiality, integrity, or availability objective.

A sufficient evidence record identifies:
- The operative declarations, assignments, comparisons, storage updates, or transitions.
- The relationships establishing the concept’s meaning.
- The flow property that depends on the concept.
- The effect relevant to the selected security objective.
- Conditions under which the claim holds.

Element names can support interpretation but require behavioral corroboration when security relevance is not explicit. A name, a hypothetical attack, or generic sensitivity is not sufficient evidence.

Check evidence against all relevant drivers, branches, resets, and updates. A favorable assignment does not establish an invariant contradicted by another reachable assignment. Resolve apparent conflicts using RTL semantics where possible. Otherwise reduce the scope of the claim to what is established or classify the affected case as Context-dependent or Insufficient evidence.

Conditional evidence may support an Established claim when the condition itself is stated by the RTL and retained in the reasoning. It cannot support an unconditional claim.

Structural classification requires its own evidence: the candidate must realize the established conceptual value through an eligible role. Mere dependency or reachability is insufficient.

8. Uncertainty

Use exactly these evidence-level names:
- Established
- Context-dependent
- Insufficient evidence

Never resolve uncertainty by assuming common hardware-security conventions.

Only Established conceptual assets and their structural references appear in the output. Context-dependent and Insufficient evidence cases are decided during analysis and are not reported.

Not established is not the same as not present. Failure to establish relevance is not evidence that an element is irrelevant.

When a concept has an Established objective but another objective remains uncertain, report only the established claims. Do not let an unsupported objective contaminate an otherwise supported classification.

9. Analysis procedure

Run the stages in this order: build the closed set; derive relationships; derive use-case flows; identify conceptual assets; identify their structural references; validate.

Start from:

Stage: Build the closed set
- Purpose: establish the only permissible structural candidates.
- Input: all supplied RTL declarations.
- Operation: inventory candidates and declaring entities according to Section 2.
- Output: a declaration inventory, entity hierarchy information, and unresolved declaration or elaboration issues.
- Dependency: no asset classification precedes this stage.

Stage: Derive relationships
- Purpose: establish behavior and distinguish realization from transport.
- Input: the inventory and RTL behavior.
- Operation: perform the data-flow, control-flow, state, and structural analyses in Section 2.
- Output: evidence-backed relationships and applicable conditions.
- Dependency: relationships refer to declared elements without promoting them to assets.

Stage: Derive use-case flows

- Input: the relationship model.
- Operation: construct the flow descriptions specified in Section 3.
- Output: RTL-grounded flows and their meaningful values, states, decisions, and events.
- Dependency: no conceptual asset is inferred merely from the declaration inventory.

Stage: Identify conceptual assets
- Purpose: identify security-relevant meanings, independently of their locations.
- Input: flows and their supporting relationships.
- Operation: apply Sections 4–8, distinguish meanings, evaluate objectives, and assign evidence levels.
- Output: fill "module name" and add an entry for each Established conceptual asset, leaving "related structural assets" empty.

Determine the module name from the supplied declaration and instantiation structure. Use the exact declared name of the unambiguous containing root. If the RTL does not establish a single root, use the declared root names, verbatim in source order and separated by "; ", as the analysis-unit label. If no root can be established, use the declared entity names in that form. Do not silently select a presumed intended top. Analyze the supplied entities regardless of this naming decision.

If a conceptual asset has multiple Established objectives, preserve its identity rather than duplicating it solely for the scalar objective field. Select the most directly evidenced objective; break an otherwise unresolved tie in schema order: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. Mention other Established objectives in "reasoning" if applicable. This is a representation rule, not a security-priority ranking.

Stage: Identify structural references
- Purpose: map Established concepts to primary realization points.
- Input: conceptual entries, the closed set, and the relationship model.
- Operation: trace each concept across its semantic representations and test candidate roles.
- Output: populate each "related structural assets" list with supported primary references only.

Apply these role tests:
- "stores": the element actually retains the conceptual value or state. Storage remains a realization even when the retained value is subsequently forwarded.
- "sets": the element establishes the conceptual value, state, decision, or event at an origin rather than merely copying or enabling an existing value.
- "computes": the element holds the result of behavior that derives the conceptual value or decision. Do not treat identity copying or routing as computation merely because it uses an expression.
- "exit port": the declared port exports the established conceptual value from its own entity, including a supplied subentity. Apply this explicit boundary rule even if the port is driven by a relay.

Input direction alone is not a realization role. An input-only port is not an exit port and does not qualify merely because a meaningful value enters through it. Bidirectional ports qualify as exit ports only for evidenced outward behavior.

Exclude pure carriers, propagation gates, and elements that merely influence the concept. A gating decision may have its own structural references only if it has independently passed conceptual-asset identification. Do not confuse that decision with the gated value.

For overlapping roles, use "exit port", then "stores", then "computes", then "sets" as the serialization precedence. This does not deny other evidenced roles.

Record fields and containing records must each independently satisfy the realization test; do not add a parent solely because it contains a qualifying field. Shared realizations may appear under different concepts when each mapping is supported.

If no eligible reference is found, recheck the mapping and preserve only what the definitions support. Never fabricate a reference or promote a carrier to fill a list.

Stage: Validate
- Purpose: ensure conceptual and structural claims satisfy every rule.
- Input: the populated object, declaration inventory, relationships, and evidence records.
- Operation: apply Section 10 and correct unsupported or inconsistent entries.
- Output: the completed object in Section 11’s format.

Filling happens during analysis. The response contains only the final completed object.

10. Validation

Validate each reported conceptual asset:
- Its semantic meaning is identifiable from the RTL.
- It belongs to a derived use-case flow.
- Its objective has a sufficient evidence chain.
- The reasoning states the relevant behavior and flow consequence rather than a generic sensitivity claim.
- Necessary conditions are preserved.
- No claim depends on comments, missing implementations, presumed application purpose, or another unsupported external premise.
- Relevant contradictory behavior has been addressed.
- The concept was not created by starting from a signal classification.

Validate each structural reference:
- "asset rtl" is an exact name from the closed set.
- "entity" is the verbatim name of the declaring entity, not an instance or receiving entity.
- The conceptual value is actually realized there.
- "realization" matches evidenced behavior and the serialization rule.
- The reference is not merely a carrier, gate, operand dependency, or potential fault influence.
- An exit-port claim concerns a port through which the value leaves its own entity.
- Field spelling and scope are faithful to the declarations.

Validate consistency and coverage:
- Trace all established realization points without extending the candidate universe.
- Do not omit supplied entities because only a top-level boundary was considered.
- Do not conflate distinct meanings or duplicate a concept solely for another location or objective.
- Repeated mappings have a distinct supported role or conceptual association, not accidental duplication.
- Empty reference lists have been rechecked and are not populated speculatively.
- No secondary asset, rejected case, vulnerability assessment, or uncertainty inventory appears in the output.

Finally validate JSON syntax, exact keys, allowed scalar values, module naming, and the absence of extra output. Apply no target, quota, or expected amount of assets.

Return the following schema and nothing else. The "reasoning" field is a concise evidence summary, not private chain-of-thought.

Return ONE JSON object and nothing else: no markdown, no code fences, no prose. Use the keys exactly as written.

    ]}
 ]}

If a single root is not established, use the declared root names verbatim in source order, separated by "; ", as the analysis-unit label. If no root can be established, use the declared entity names in that form. Do not guess the intended top. This naming decision does not remove any supplied entity from analysis.

For a concept with multiple Established objectives, preserve its identity instead of duplicating it solely to fit the scalar field. Select the most directly evidenced objective. Break an otherwise unresolved tie in schema order: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. Include other Established objectives in "reasoning" if applicable. This is a serialization choice, not a security ranking.

Stage: Identify structural references
Purpose: locate primary realizations of Established concepts.
Input: conceptual entries, the closed set, and the relationship model.
Operation: trace each concept’s supported semantic representations and test candidate roles.
Output: fill each "related structural assets" list with eligible references only.

Use these realization tests:
- "stores": the candidate actually retains the conceptual value or state. A state-holding element remains a realization even if it later forwards that value.
- "sets": the candidate establishes the conceptual value, state, decision, or event at an origin, rather than merely copying or enabling an existing value.
- "computes": the candidate holds the result of behavior that derives the conceptual value or decision. An expression that merely copies, routes, or gates an existing value is not computation of that asset.
- "exit port": the declared port exports the established conceptual value from the entity that declares it. This applies to supplied subentities as well as the containing entity, even when the port is driven by a relay.

Apply the exit-port rule at actual entity boundaries. Do not reject a genuine exit port merely because its driver is pass-through logic.

An input-only port is not an exit port and does not qualify merely because a meaningful value enters through it. It must independently satisfy an evidenced realization role. For a bidirectional port, establish outward behavior before using "exit port".

Exclude elements that only transport or gate the value between realization points. Also exclude elements that merely influence it or could violate its objective. These are secondary assets unless they independently realize an Established concept.

A selector or gating decision is not a realization of the value it controls. It can be reported for its own meaning only after independently passing conceptual identification and evidence checks.

When an element satisfies overlapping roles for a concept, select "exit port", then "stores", then "computes", then "sets" as the serialization precedence. Do not interpret this precedence as denying other supported roles.

Evaluate a record and its fields independently. Do not include a containing record solely because a field qualifies. An element can appear under different concepts when each semantic mapping is supported.

If no eligible reference is found, recheck the mapping. Do not fabricate a reference or promote a carrier to populate a list.

Stage: Validate
Purpose: enforce evidentiary, structural, and output correctness.
Input: the populated object and supporting inventory and relationships.
Operation: apply Section 10, correcting unsupported or inconsistent entries.
Output: the final completed object.

Perform this filling during analysis. Return only the completed object, not intermediate versions.

10. Validation

Check each conceptual entry:
- The concept is an identifiable RTL value, state, decision, or event.
- A derived use-case flow depends on it.
- The selected security objective follows from a sufficient evidence chain.
- The reasoning identifies concrete RTL behavior and its flow consequence.
- Relevant conditions and limitations are preserved.
- No claim depends on comments, an assumed application, unstated requirements, missing implementation behavior, or other external premises.
- Relevant contradictory behavior has been resolved conservatively.
- The concept was identified before its structural references.

Check each structural reference:
- "asset rtl" is an exact name from the closed set.
- "entity" is the verbatim declaring entity name, not an instance name or another entity connected to it.
- The element actually realizes the stated conceptual meaning.
- "realization" is supported by behavior and follows the role serialization rule.
- The element is not merely a carrier, propagation gate, dependency, operand influence, or potential fault source.
- An exit-port claim concerns outward propagation from that port’s own entity.
- Internal record fields use their correct declared dotted names.

Check the object as a whole:
- Established realization points have been traced without enlarging the closed set.
- All supplied entities were considered.
- Different meanings were not conflated.
- The same concept was not duplicated solely for different locations or objectives.
- Repeated references are supported by their conceptual mappings rather than accidental duplication.
- Empty reference lists were rechecked and were not populated speculatively.
- Context-dependent cases, Insufficient evidence cases, secondary assets, vulnerability assessments, and uncertainty inventories are absent.
- The module label follows the supplied declaration structure.
- Keys, scalar values, and JSON syntax match the schema.

Do not impose a target, quota, threshold, percentage, or expected amount of assets, concepts, or elements.
